Nightmare on the Crow

Ten different looks for your new website. Each one is a complete, working homepage with the same information, your four nights, your $20 / $15-with-a-donation pricing, Kids Day, the food-shelf drive, and the story of three neighbors, presented ten different ways. 01

The Greenfield Gazette

The whole site is a small-town newspaper reporting on what's gone wrong on Harff Road. Your attractions become news stories and classified ads, and the price runs as a clip-out coupon.

Try: hover the blacked-out words to un-redact them.
View full site →
02

Blackout

The page loads pitch black and the visitor's cursor is a flashlight. Things hide in the dark around the words, and one of them doesn't stay put. A "Lights On" button for anyone who'd rather not.

Try: move your mouse slowly around the edges of the page.
View full site →
03

The Murder

A living flock of crows drifts behind the entire site and scatters when you click. Huge headlines, a scrolling ticker of your dates, and the most "big-budget" feel of the ten.

Try: click anywhere to startle the flock.
View full site →
04

Survive the Trail

A hand-drawn map of the property with seven numbered stops. Tap each one to read a note from the crew about what happens there. It answers "what do I get for $20?" better than any paragraph could.

Try: find all seven stops on the map.
View full site →
05

Tape 09

Recovered camcorder footage from the trail. Scanlines, tracking glitches, a blinking REC light and a tape-counter countdown. Built to get shared by teens and twenty-somethings.

Try: the EFFECTS toggle in the corner turns the static off.
View full site →
06

The Bonfire

The closest to your current orange-and-black look, done at a premium level. Rising embers, drifting fog, a giant countdown to opening night and one glowing ticket button. The safe, proven choice.

Try: scroll and watch the nav shrink to a Tickets pill.
View full site →
07

Walk It

Scrolling the page is walking the trail. Eight scenes, from the headlights on Harff Road through the tunnels, the corn and the graveyard, to a dawn exit where all the practical info lives.

Try: scroll slowly and watch the barn window.
View full site →
08

Lights On / Lights Off

One big light switch in the header. Off is the haunt: dark and orange. On is the same site re-done for Kids Day parents: sunny, friendly, and the crow gets a tiny hat. Both audiences, one website.

Try: flip the switch.
View full site →
09

The Scarecrow's Almanac

A farmers' almanac for the October nights, written by the scarecrow who watches the corn. Woodcut illustrations, a real calendar of your four nights, and an FAQ answered in the scarecrow's voice. The most handmade and neighborly of the ten.

Try: the scarecrow watches your cursor.
View full site →
10

Harff Road: A Text Adventure

The homepage is a playable game. Type or tap your way down the trail and learn the dates, prices and Kids Day along the way. A "skip to the boring part" link drops anyone straight to a clean info page.

Try: type ride the wagon, then check my wallet.

Like parts of different ones?

That works too. The final website can combine them: one concept's look, another's countdown, another's wording. The pricing, dates and community story travel to any of them.
