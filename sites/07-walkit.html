Chapter 1 — The Wagon

Three neighbors. One bad idea.

It started with three people on the same road who loved Halloween a little too much and wanted an excuse to give something back. A hayride into the woods seemed harmless. That was the first mistake. The wagon still runs every night — it's how you get in. Getting out is your problem.

Chapter 2 — The Trail

The trees close in.

The trail winds through real forest near the Crow River — narrow, dark, and longer than it looked from the wagon. The path bends, the fog settles, and whatever is keeping pace with you off the trail doesn't seem to mind the brush. This isn't just a haunt. It's an experience designed to crawl under your skin.

Chapter 3 — The Tunnels

You can't see the end.

The trail drops into tunnels and winding passages where the only light is the one you're standing under. Stay close. Count your group before you go in — and count again when you come out.

Chapter 4 — The Buildings

Somebody's home.

Scattered through the woods are haunted buildings full of live actors and hand-built displays — the Butcher, the Bus, the Barn Hang, the Saloon. The windows are lit. That isn't for your benefit.

Chapter 5 — The Corn

Rows you don't remember entering.

The eerie corn maze is the part people warn their friends about. Dark Harvest, then the Corn Field: the stalks are taller than you, every row looks like the last one, and the scarecrow was facing the other way a second ago.

Chapter 6 — The Graveyard

Ben's corner.

Our set designer Ben builds the Graveyard by hand every season — the zombies clawing out of the ground, the Camp beyond it, and the skeleton trapped in the Cage who has been trying to get your attention since the wagon dropped you off. Every scare on this trail is a neighbor in makeup. Don't let that make you feel better.

Chapter 7 — The Exit

You made it out.

Breathe. Grab something from the food trucks, tell your friends you weren't scared, and scroll on for the practical part — dates, prices, and how bringing a donation saves you five bucks.

FAQ

Things people ask before they get on the wagon.

How scary is it, really?
Scary. Live actors, tight spaces, tunnels, dark forest, and a corn maze that plays with your sense of direction. It's designed to crawl under your skin, not just make you jump. If you want the full experience with none of the fear, that's what Kids Day is for.
Is there an age recommendation?
We don't set a hard age limit — you know your kid better than we do. Younger or nervous kids should come to Kids Day (Sat Oct 10, Noon–3 PM), where the lights are on and the actors are out of makeup. Teens and adults are the evening crowd.
What if it rains?
A little rain makes the woods better, and we plan to be out there. If lightning or severe weather forces a change, we post it on Facebook and Instagram first — check before you drive out.
Where do I park?
Parking is on site at 6470 Harff Road. Follow the signs and the crew with the flashlights, and leave nothing valuable in view.
How long does it take?
Long enough. It's a hayride, a forest trail, tunnels, several buildings, and a corn maze — plan on a good stretch in the woods, and arrive well before last entry at 10 PM so you're not rushed.
Will the actors touch me? Can I touch them?
Our actors won't touch you, and we ask the same of you. They're neighbors doing this for free; the props are handmade. Hands to yourself and everyone has a better night.
Is the trail wheelchair or stroller accessible?
It's a real forest trail — dirt, roots, hills, and tunnels — so it's tough going for wheelchairs and strollers. Message us before you come and we'll talk through what's possible for your group.
Is there food?
Yes. Just in Time Concessions and Burger Box are on site. Eat before or after the trail — nobody wants to carry fries through the corn.
