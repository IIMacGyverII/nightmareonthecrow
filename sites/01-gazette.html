Front Page · Special Report

Something Stirs on the Crow

Lights seen in the trees off Harff Road. A wagon leaves at dusk and comes back lighter. The three neighbors responsible decline to comment beyond: “It all begins with a hayride.”

By The Editors · Greenfield, Minn. · Filed after dark

GREENFIELD — For four nights this October, the woods along the Crow River will not be quiet. A hayride departs from 6470 Harff Road at 7:30 PM sharp. Where it goes after that is a matter for the sheriff.

What this paper can print: a haunted trail winds through the forest — tunnels, winding paths, buildings full of live actors and displays, and an eerie corn maze that has stopped returning people. The neighbors who built it describe it as “an experience designed to crawl under your skin.” The Gazette describes it as the only reason to leave the house in October.

“This isn’t just a haunt,” they insist. We attempted to verify. Our correspondent entered the tree line at 7:45 and was recovered near the gate at 10:00. She has declined to be interviewed, but she did buy a ticket for the following Friday.

Admission is $20 at the gate — or $15 for anyone who brings a canned good or a winter clothing item. Every donation goes to the food shelf and winter clothing drive run with the Hanover Fire Department. Other haunts in the metro charge $30 to $50 and bury the number three clicks deep. Ours is on the front page, above the fold, in the price column. (Continued on Page 2, if Page 2 can be found.)

Page A2 · What to expect on the trail, as reported to this office

Transportation

It All Begins with a Hayride

The wagon departs from the gate and delivers you to the tree line. It does not wait, and it does not take requests. Passengers are advised that the ride is the last comfortable seat of the evening and to enjoy it accordingly.

Where the wagon stops, the trail starts — at the Bridge, past a man in a striped suit answering to Beetlejuice. From there you are on foot, in the dark, in a forest that has been rehearsing.

Lost & Found

LOST: One skeleton, last seen in a cage.

Answers to nothing. Cage was locked. Cage is still locked. Set designer Ben states he “built the cage himself” and “has questions.” Reward: none. If found, do not return to sender. Do not make eye contact. Do not stand near the cage for long, either.

Public Notice

Regarding the Tunnels

The township wishes residents to know the Tunnel and the winding paths through the Last Woods are perfectly safe. Markers are clearly placed. Markers may occasionally be moved by something with hands.

Guests are asked to stay on the path, keep moving, and not investigate any sound that seems to be investigating them.

Real Estate

FOR SALE: Corn maze. Never returns anything.

Eerie. Good bones. Sold as-is. Prior guests not included. Comes with the Corn Field and a working Dark Harvest. Rows are taller than you and quieter than they should be. Serious inquiries only — we can hear you either way.

Inspection Report

Haunted Buildings Open for Inspection

Every structure on the trail — the Butcher, the Saloon, the Barn Hang — has been found to be fully occupied, by live actors and displays. The Graveyard behind them remains under Ben’s personal management and is, in this paper’s opinion, over capacity.

Doors close behind you. That is how doors work.

Missing

MISSING: One quiet night.

Last seen October 8. Interactive scares since reported at every turn of the trail — things that reach, things that follow, things that wait for you to look away. If located, please do not return it before October 18. We are using the dark.

Op-Ed · Page B2

Why We Do This

By the three neighbors of Harff Road

We started this because we loved Halloween and we loved giving back — in that order some years, and the reverse in others. Somewhere along the way the two things became the same thing. You come out to Greenfield, you get scared, and a can of soup ends up on a food-shelf pantry. That’s the whole business model.

“Every $15 ticket is a can on a shelf or a coat on a kid at a January bus stop. We’ll take that trade every night we’re open.”

We run a food shelf drive and a winter clothing drive at the gate every night. Hats, gloves, coats, non-perishables — bring what you can and we knock five dollars off. We do it with the Hanover Fire Department, who have been good to us and to this town for a long time. They’ve got our backs. We’ve got your screams.

We’re not a corporate haunt. There’s no franchise, no ticket surge, no forty-dollar “VIP fast pass.” It’s three neighbors, a crew of volunteers, a set designer who cannot stop building things, and a forest that was already a little strange before we touched it.

So: bring something. Scream a little. Go home warm, and make sure somebody else does too.

Food Shelf Drive

Non-perishables accepted at the gate all four nights. Canned goods, boxed staples, anything with a long shelf life and a short list of ingredients.

Winter Clothing Drive

Hats, gloves and coats — new or gently worn. Minnesota winter is the scariest thing on this property and it isn’t on the trail.

Partner: Hanover Fire Department

Our community partner. Donations are collected with them and put where they’re needed.

Letters to the Editor

Page B4 · Questions from readers, answered by the people who built the trail

“How scary is it, honestly?”— Nervous in Rockford

Scary. It’s a haunted trail through a real forest at night, with tunnels, haunted buildings, live actors and a corn maze. The whole point is that it crawls under your skin — and the crew is very good at their jobs.

If you want the woods without the dread, Kids Day on Saturday, Oct. 10 runs the trail with the lights on. — Ed.

“Is it okay for kids?”— A Parent, Corcoran

The evening haunt is built to frighten, and you know your kid better than we do. If they love scary movies and beg for haunted houses, they’ll be fine with you beside them. If they’re on the fence, Kids Day (noon to 3, $5, lights on, trick-or-treating) is the answer — and the ticket is good for $5 off a night ticket when they’re ready. — Ed.

“What if it rains?”— Watching the Radar, Rogers

It’s an outdoor trail, so dress for October in Minnesota: boots, layers, a jacket you don’t mind getting muddy. Light rain just makes the woods better. If the weather turns severe enough to be unsafe, we’ll post an update on our socials before the gate opens — check there before you drive out. — Ed.

“Where do I park?”— Coming from Maple Grove

Parking is on the property at 6470 Harff Road. Follow the signs and the crew with the flashlights — they’ll point you in. Arrive a little early; the hayride line is part of the evening. — Ed.

“How long does it take?”— On a Schedule, Delano

Longer than you’d like. Shorter than it feels. Between the hayride, the trail, the tunnels and the maze, plan on making a night of it — and budget extra for the line and a stop at the food trucks. Last entry is 10 PM and the trail runs until 10:30; the last wagon goes when the last ticket goes. — Ed.

“Will the actors touch me?”— Personal Space, Buffalo

No touching — in either direction. Our actors get close, get loud and get in your way, but they keep their hands to themselves. We ask you to return the courtesy. Anyone who grabs, shoves or swings at a crew member is done for the night, no refund. They’re volunteers. Be good to them. — Ed.

“Is the trail wheelchair accessible?”— Planning Ahead, Greenfield

Honest answer: it’s a forest trail with uneven ground, tunnels and winding paths, and parts of it are not friendly to wheels. If you or someone in your group has mobility needs, message us on our socials before you come and we’ll tell you exactly what to expect and what we can do to help. Kids Day, in daylight, is the easier walk. — Ed.

“Is there food?”— Hungry, Hanover

Yes. Just in Time Concessions and Burger Box are on site. Eat before the corn maze. Nobody has ever regretted eating before the corn maze. — Ed.
