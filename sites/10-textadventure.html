Why so cheap?

Because three neighbors built this in their own woods and they'd rather fill a food shelf than a parking ramp. The $15 rate isn't a coupon — it's the point. Every can of beans and every coat goes to people in our community who need it.

The big Twin Cities haunts run $30–$50 and make you dig through a ticket vendor to find out. Ours is $20. It says so right here.

04 · What to expect

It all begins with a hayride.

This isn't just a haunt. It's an experience designed to crawl under your skin.

  • 01

    The hayride

    A wagon takes you from the road into the dark. No turning around after this.

  • 02

    The haunted trail

    A winding path through the forest along the Crow. Interactive scares. Things that move when you don't.

  • 03

    Tunnels & winding paths

    Low, close, and built to make you lose the direction of the road.

  • 04

    Haunted buildings

    Rooms full of live actors and displays. Ben's skeleton-in-a-cage lives here. So does the zombie graveyard.

  • 05

    The corn maze

    Rows taller than you, and something two rows over that matches your pace.

05 · Community give-back

Scare people. Feed people.

Three neighbors started this because they loved two things: Halloween, and giving back. The second one is why the ticket drops to $15.

Food shelf drive

Bring a non-perishable food item to the gate and save $5. The bin fills up every night.

Winter clothing drive

Hats, gloves, coats. Minnesota winter starts about ten minutes after Halloween. Same $5 off.

Hanover Fire Department

We partner with the Hanover FD so what you bring gets to the people who need it.

07 · FAQ

Questions from the porch.

How scary is it?

Scary. It's a real trail through real dark woods with live actors, tunnels, and buildings built by people who enjoy this a little too much. If you want the gentle version, come to Kids Day with the lights on.

What age is it for?

The evening trail is built for teens and adults who like being startled. Younger kids — and anyone who doesn't — should come Saturday, October 10, Noon–3 PM for Kids Day: $5, lights on, family-friendly.

What if it rains?

It's an outdoor trail in October in Minnesota. Dress for it: boots, layers, a coat you don't mind getting muddy. Check our Facebook or Instagram on the day for any weather updates.

Where do I park?

On site at 6470 Harff Road. Follow the signs and the crew when you pull in. Bring a flashlight for the walk back to the car — or don't, if you like a challenge.

How long does it take?

Budget about an hour for the hayride out, the trail, and a stop at the food trucks. Your pace decides the rest. Running is allowed.

Will the actors touch me?

No. The actors won't touch you, and you don't touch the actors. They're your neighbors. Be cool.

Is it wheelchair accessible?

The trail is a natural forest path with tunnels, uneven ground, and a corn maze, so it's not wheelchair accessible. Message us before you come and we'll talk through what's possible, especially for Kids Day.

Is there food?

Yes. Just in Time Concessions and Burger Box park on site. Bring a few extra dollars — the trail burns calories.
