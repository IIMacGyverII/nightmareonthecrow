002

What waits on the trail

Six stops · No safe word

This isn't just a haunt. It's an experience designed to crawl under your skin. A wagon drops you at the edge of the woods off Harff Road and the forest does the rest: winding paths, tunnels, buildings that should be empty and aren't, and a corn maze that only pretends to have an exit.

Photo: trail entrance at dusk

  1. It all begins with a hayride. Climb on, sit close, and try not to count the things watching from the treeline. The wagon goes in. Whether it comes back out with the same headcount is between you and the woods.

  2. A trail that winds through real forest on real ground: roots, ruts, and switchbacks lit just enough to see what you'd rather not. Every turn was walked, wired, and rehearsed by neighbors who know exactly where you'll look.

  3. Low ceilings. Long stretches with no light at all. Sound travels strangely down here, so the scream you hear ahead of you might be your own group. Might not.

  4. Structures full of live actors and hand-built scenes: Ben's zombie graveyard, the skeleton trapped in the cage, rooms that look wrong before you understand why. Doors close behind you. That's the whole idea.

  5. Stalks taller than you, rows tighter than they look, and a rustling that keeps pace with you no matter how fast you walk. The maze has an exit. Finding it is the attraction.

  6. The crew is your neighbors: volunteers who spend their October nights in the cold and the makeup because scaring you is the best part of their year. They don't touch. They don't need to.

The scare is the fundraiser

Food shelf · Winter clothing · Hanover FD

Three neighbors started this because they loved Halloween and loved giving back, in that order on paper and the other order in practice. Every ticket, every canned good, every coat left at the gate goes to people in the west metro who need it.

A

Food Shelf Drive

Bring a non-perishable food item and your trail ticket drops to $15. The bins fill up fast on Saturdays. Help us make that a problem.

B

Winter Clothing Drive

Hats, gloves, coats: anything that keeps a neighbor warm through a Minnesota winter counts as a donation and earns the same $5 off.

C

Hanover Fire Department

We partner with the Hanover Fire Department. When you buy a ticket, part of what you're paying for is the people who show up when the sirens go.

008

Questions from the brave

Eight answers · Zero comfort
Q1How scary is it, actually?+
Scary. It's a haunted trail through dark woods with tunnels, buildings full of live actors, and a corn maze. It's designed to crawl under your skin, not to be walked through with your eyes closed. If you want the same woods without the nightmares, that's what Kids Day is for.
Q2Is there an age recommendation?+
We don't set a hard age limit; you know your kid better than we do. The evening trail is intense, dark, and loud. For younger kids, bring them to Kids Day on Saturday, Oct 10, from noon to 3 PM: lights on, trick-or-treating, and the crew out of costume.
Q3What if it rains?+
We run in the rain. Mud is part of the trail's charm. If weather turns unsafe (lightning, high wind), we'll post updates on Facebook and Instagram before gates open. Dress for October in Minnesota either way: layers, closed-toe shoes you don't love.
Q4Where do I park?+
On site at 6470 Harff Road. Follow the crew's lights when you pull in. It's a field, so give yourself a minute and don't block the wagon lane.
Q5How long does it take?+
Budget an evening. Between the hayride, the trail, the tunnels, the buildings, and the maze, it isn't a sprint, and the woods move at their own pace. Last entry is 10 PM, so arrive earlier if you want time for the food trucks.
Q6Will the actors touch me?+
No. Our actors don't touch guests, and we ask the same of you. Hands off the crew, the sets, and the things in the cages. Anyone who can't manage that will be walked out, and not the fun way.
Q7Is the trail wheelchair accessible?+
Honest answer: the trail is natural forest ground with roots, ruts, mud, and tunnels, and it isn't paved. Reach out before you visit and we'll talk through what we can realistically do for your group, including Kids Day, when the lights are on and the pace is slower.
Q8Is there food?+
Yes. Just in Time Concessions and Burger Box are on site. Eat before the tunnels or after. Not during.

Still not sure?

Message us on Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok. A real neighbor answers, usually the one who isn't currently wearing a mask.
